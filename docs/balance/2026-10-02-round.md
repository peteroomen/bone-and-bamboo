# Bone & Bamboo: round simulator results (2 October 2026)

`roundsim.py` plays one round: hand of tiles, a face-up wall of stacks, play one set or discard
up to 5 per turn, refill from the stack tops, and the whole table scores at the end
(chips x mult). Two bots: **greedy** plays the best set it has; **pongs** holds pairs and digs
for pongs. Bots are not human forecasts; they show what the rules allow and what moves the numbers.

## Recommended starting rules

- Tiles: 3 suits, 1-9, 3 copies each (81). Honours, 4th copies and flowers are bought.
- Hand 8, 8 stacks, see the top tile and 1 under it (seeing 2 adds 5%, 3 adds nothing; keep deeper sight for a curio).
- 8 plays, 3 discards (5 only helps a pong build). 10 plays if rounds should be longer (score x1.55, 86 taps).
- Prices: pair 5/+1, chow 10/+1, pong 40/+4, kong 100/+8. With these, chasing pongs is about even with playing chows, so both are real strategies.
- East target about 2,000 (the greedy bot's base median is 2,450). Later targets need the run simulator (shops, curios).

## Findings

1. Chows dominate: about 6 of 8 plays at any price, because a chow needs three different tiles and a pong needs every copy of one.
2. Pongs stay rare with 3 copies (1.7 a round even when hunting). Buying 4th copies is what makes a pong build pull ahead (+35%).
3. Score grows with the square of the plays (more sets add chips and mult). Plays are the strongest lever; discards and wall depth are weak ones.
4. Seeing 1 tile under each top is the big step (+24% over seeing only the tops). Fewer stacks hurt (5 stacks: -11%).
5. Flowers as written are far too strong: a level-up applies to every chow on the table. Two flowers nearly double the score and make it swingy. Make them smaller.
6. Curio thresholds need the sim: "x3 with 4+ pongs" never fires; All Simples only fires once 1s and 9s are removed, and then doubles the score (the deck-shaping loop works).
7. A round is about 70 taps (3 to 5 minutes).
